# INTRODUCTION

The process of reforming gases and reducing the iron ore is carried out in the reduction area by means of reducing gases at high pressure and high temperatures. The reactor, the heater and the reduction circuit compressor are the main equipment that forms this area and together they contribute to the production of the DRI.

At the reduction circuit, the reforming gases and reducing gases are adapted to the conditions of pressure, temperature and quality required by the process. This gases, which are mainly formed by hydrogen, methane and carbon monoxide after being adapted they enter the reactor and in this equipment is where the reforming and reductions reactions are carried out. By means of these reducing agents, the oxygen is eliminated from the iron mineral, this way we get DRI.

In this module, an explanation of the reduction flow diagram including its operation parameters is presented. A description of the reduction compressor will be given, along with its parts, its operation data sheets and instrumentation; fundamental concepts, the equipment used in the circuit and its control loops as well as the interlocks.

The start up procedure, normal operation and shut down procedure, along with the principal failures that can occur during the reduction circuit operation also will be described in this module.

<a id="system-basic-concepts"></a>
# I. SYSTEM BASIC CONCEPTS

The reduction circuit function is to give the process gas the suitable conditions of pressure, temperature and flow, as well as its composition to reduce the iron ores and simultaneously promote reforming reactions within the reduction zone of the reactor.

The reduction zone is the superior cylindrical part of the reactor; which is covered internally with insulating material and refractory brick. This zone receives the iron ores through the superior dome of the reactor, which flows towards the inferior part by gravity in countercurrent with the reducing gas flow.

Here, a quite complex phenomenon appears: the heat and mass transference happen simultaneously. The heat is provided by the hot process gas that enters the reactor by the low part of the reduction zone. The gas is equally distributed around the reduction zone by a circular plenum. Around the plenum rectangular nozzles are located, radially aimed with a very slight slope downwards, whose function is to distribute in uniform form the process gas in that sectional area.

The gas flows upwards in countercurrent with the solid up to the superior part; or reactor dome, of the reduction area, here the gas is collected and leaves the reactor through the reduction outlet.

Because the process is continuous and in steady state, all the phenomena that happens within the reduction zone is independent of the time, but dependant of the position.

When the iron ores enters the reactor, it makes contact whit the process gas which at this point is at its lower potential and lower temperature; here the heating of the mineral is just starting. As soon as the solid begins moving down by gravity, its temperature begins to increase until reaching the temperature at which it starts reducing, its speed is controlled by a rotary valve which is located at the inferior part of the reactor.

When the mineral reaches a determined temperature, it begins to be reduced by hydrogen and carbon monoxide. As the temperature of the mineral increases the rate of reduction does too. Reduction is controlled by temperature; up to here the quality of the reducing gases is not the main parameter for reduction.

An external layer, which surrounds the iron ore surface, makes it difficult for the hydrogen and carbon monoxide to reach the interior of each pellet. In addition to this, the gaseous products like CO<sub>2</sub> and H<sub>2</sub>O, which are molecules bigger than the reactants, difficult the reduction reactions. At this height of the reactor, the pellet temperature is relatively high, but the temperature in this case does not controls the reduction of the iron mineral, in this case, reduction is controlled by the diffusion of the reactants and products within the solid.

As the pellet crosses the reduction zone, the hydrogen and carbon monoxide concentration increases, favoring the diffusion of the reagents within the mineral.

At the same time, inside the reactor; the gaseous reactions of reformation happen and are generated in two places: the pre-reforming of the process gas is carried out at the transference line by means of an oxygen injection causing a partial combustion; and the complete reforming takes place at the reduction zone of the reactor since the DRI acts like a catalyst for these reactions.

This way, while in the transference line the reducing gas is pre-reformed and its temperature rises, inside the reactor the iron mineral reduction is taking place, contributing to the DRI formation, which acts as a catalyst for the completion of the reforming reactions. In addition of the methane reformation and water gas shift other reactions like carburization take place, these reactions have a direct relation with the composition of the process gas.

The exhaust gas leaves the reduction zone and enters the heat recuperator where it yields great part of its sensible heat to the process stream gas, later exhaust gas enters to the quench orifice where it continues its stage of cooling and then enters the venturi where all the solids are removed. Later the exhaust gas stream passes through separator to continue with its cleaning and later it enters to a quench tower where its stage of cooling finishes.

Next the exhaust gas passes through a K.O. drum where the water is eliminated of the stream gas, then exhaust gas is filtered and then it is recycled by the gas compressor and its discharge passes through a after-cooler where the process gas temperature drops and the water is condensed, later it goes to a K.O. drum where the water particles are separates and retained.

The exhaust gas cooled is divided in two streams one enters to the CO<sub>2</sub> adsorption system, the second stream enters to the CO<sub>2</sub> absorption system, both decarbonated process gas outlets join in a single stream an then the decarbonated process gas is mixed with a natural gas make up. The process gas mixed enters to the humidifier where the process gas humidity is incremented and then the process gas stream enters to the heat recuperator and takes the heat from the exhaust reduction gas; increasing its temperature. The process gas stream leaves from the heat recuperator and passes through the process gas heater and before entering the reactor its temperature is risen even more by means of an oxygen injection in the transference line, this way the reduction circuit is completed.

There are other connections to the circuit like the sulphur injections which is located at the heater inlet; at the quench tower outlet there is a external cooling system derivation; between the dollinger filters and the compressor suction there is a line that corresponds to the outlet exhaust gas to the tail gas header which control the reactor pressure; at the after-cooler outlet there is another cool process gas outlet to the reactor cone and to the external cooling system; at the exit of the CO<sub>2</sub> absorption system there is a gas by-pass to cool the charge ducts; and at the end of the quench orifice there is a venting line of the reactor.

In addition; to control the process gas going into to the heater there is a re-circulation line that connects the compressor discharge to the cooling quench tower. And also the reactor has a process gas by pass after the oxygen injection that enters to the quench orifice of the dummy quench line, then passes through a K.O. drum and connects to the outlet gas line of the separator.

Other connections are the process gas outlet to the compressor discharge that feed to the pneumatic transport system, which also feeds process gas to the H<sub>2</sub>S injection system, and plus the reactor venting line connects to the quench orifice outlet.

# II. SYSTEM DESCRIPTION AND EQUIPMENT

The reduction circuit of the HYL 4M plant works under the HYL ZR scheme, which has the particularity of not having a reformer, this scheme combines the two conventional zones that conform the HYL direct reduction process (reforming and reduction), into one. So is in this circuit that the reformation, reduction and carburization; to produce the DRI at the conditions required by the client, happen.

Natural gas, as well as in the conventional process, is used for reformation. In the process without reformer, the reducing gas is generated in two places inside the reduction circuit. The first place where the reforming or pre reforming reactions are carried out, is in the transference line, here the partial combustion of natural gas takes place using a oxygen injection. Such reactions are the following:

CH<sub>4</sub> + ½ O<sub>2</sub> 2H<sub>2</sub> + CO + 36 kJ/mole

CH<sub>4</sub> + 2O<sub>2</sub> CO<sub>2</sub> + 2H<sub>2</sub>O + 802 kJ/mole

CO + H<sub>2</sub>O CO<sub>2</sub> + H<sub>2</sub> + 41 kJ/mole

The previous reactions show the generation of reducing gas by means of oxygen use.

The second place where the reforming reactions are carried out is in the reactor reduction zone, because the DRI that is in this zone acts as a catalyst for these reactions. This way, while is the transference line the gas is pre reformed, inside the reactor the reduction reactions are carried out, contributing on the formation of DRI as a catalyst. The reforming reactions are the following:

CH<sub>4</sub> + H<sub>2</sub>O → 3<sub>2</sub> + CO - 206 kJ/mole

CO + H<sub>2</sub>O → CO<sub>2</sub> + H<sub>2</sub> + 41 kJ/mole

CH<sub>4</sub> + CO<sub>2</sub> → 2CO + 2H<sub>2</sub> - 247 kJ/mole

The previous reactions are endothermic as a whole, reason why they require energy to take place.

## A. REDUCTION CIRCUIT FLOW DIAGRAM

The reduction area function of the HYL 4M plant is to reform “in-situ” the natural gas, and obtain hot DRI to be pneumatically transported to he melt shop or to the external cooling system.

To carry out its function the reduction area requires a conditioning of the recycled gas. This conditioning consists of; cooling, cleaning, compressing and purifying this gas, plus an injection of natural gas, water and DMDS to the recycled gas. It is also necessary to pre heat the mixture, have a partial combustion of the mixture with oxygen, before entering the reactor.

In addition, the reduction circuit has a process gas by pass line after the oxygen injection to the cooling quench tower. This line is known as dummy quench. This line has a process gas quench tower (PG-650-C) and a process gas K.O. drum (PG-650-F). The dummy quench is mainly used during the start up while pre-reforming the first gases by the partial combustion and/or during a shut down where is not required to let pass hot gas through the reactor.

The reduction circuit description is the following:

The process gas that comes out of the process gas heater (PG-601-B) between 910-930°C, receives an oxygen injection to increase the temperature to 1065°C and this way initiate the natural gas pre reforming.

The pre reformed natural gas enters the reactor (RE-621-D) with a 5.3 kg/cm2 (a). The natural gas finishes reforming in-situ and reduces the iron at the reactor reduction zone. The iron ore flows downwards by gravity and the gas flows in countercurrent through the iron ore bed. The exhaust gas leaves through the top section of the reactor with a temperature between 410-440ºC with high water content, CO2 and fines due to the H2 y CO reactions and the fines drag.

The reactor exhaust gas is conditioned to be recycled to the reactor this conditioning is next described.

Immediately at the reactor outlet the exhaust gas passes through the gas–gas heat recuperator (PG-614-C) where the exhaust gas receives the first cooling stage giving up part of its energy to the process gas that is going in to the heater (PG-601-B), the exhaust gas leaves the heat recuperator at 265°C.

This exhaust gas stream is passes to a quench orifice (PG-611-C) where it receives its second cooling stage by direct water contact; the gas leaves within 105-110°C temperature.

Then the exhaust gas passes through the venturi (PG-630-L) where it changes its speed, due to the venturi diameter reduction, with this change of speed the solid is removed form the gas stream, then the venturi outlet flow enters through the separator (PG-630-F), here solids and water are removed from the gas by gravity, at the separator bottom , the gas flows upwards through a Chevron grill which removes the solids that the gas stills carries, this grill receives a 5 Ton/Hr water flow to avoid obstruction, after the grill the gas goes through a demisting pad which removes the smallest particles from the gas stream.

The separator PG-630-F feed the exhaust gas to a second venturi (PG-631-L) which separates small solid particles from the exhaust gas stream; solids of greater weight are created due to the change of speed and water injection making the separation easier.

The exhaust gas without and important solid level passes through a third cooling stage, at the exhaust gas cooling quench tower (PG-631-E). Here the gas is redirected through an internal elbow to impact the water at the quench tower bottom of the water level, the gas on its way up goes through a packed bed of flexigrid which enhances the contact surface between the gas and the water which is injected above the packed bed, managing to cool the gas to a temperature of 35-45°C, the exhaust gas before leaving the quench tower passes through a demisting pad which removes water that could be in the gas stream.

The exhaust gas stream is passed through a K.O. drum (PG671-F) to ensure that the water in the exhaust gas has been removed, at the superior zone of the K.O. drum there is a demisting pad.

Once the exhaust gas is cooled and dried, its filtered through a filter (PG-671-G) which can remove particles greater to 5 microns, this is to avoid compressor damage, there are 2 filters that operate in parallel, the filters have a by pass to be changed when they reach a droop pressure of 200 mm of water column.

At the filter exhaust gas outlet, an exhaust gas stream is released from the system, this way it is managed to control the reduction system pressure and the concentration of reducing gases is avoided, and the other exhaust gas stream is fed to the recycle gas compressor (PG-671-J) to increase its pressure and its flow. The compressor discharge conditions are 7.4 kg/cm<sup>2</sup> abs. y 99°C this due to compression of gas.

The compressed exhaust gas heats up due to compression work, to avoid the problems that condensation could cause at the VPSA it is required to cool the gas at the compressor outlet at a temperature that minimize condensation, for that a after-cooler is used (PG-671-C) where the exhaust gas temperature is reduced from 99 to 42°C.

Once the exhaust gas is cooled, it is passed through a K.O. drum (PG-622-F) where the condensation that the exhaust gas could have is removed and this way preventing liquids going into the VPSA.

Due to CO reactions, the exhaust gas leaves the reactor with CO2 content between 12-13.0 %. Compressed and cold exhaust gas is divided in two streams; one enters the VPSA CO2 adsorption plant (CO-635-D1/8) where it is adsorbed to 4.5-5.0% of CO2 at the stream outlet, second exhaust gas stream enters to the CO2 absorption system where it is adsorbed to 1.0 % of CO2 at the stream outlet. The exhausted gas with a low percentage of CO2 (3.2-3.5%) and totally dry (because the adsorber removes all the water) receives an injection of natural gas as make up, this mixture is the process gas and after this natural gas injection it passes through a humidifier (PG-622-E), where, with a water injection at 82°C the humidity percent to is raised up to 4.6%. The process gas enters the gas–gas heat recuperator (PG-614-C) from the shell side to increase its temperature to 275°C.

The pre heated process gas is injected with sulphur (25 ppm) to minimize metal dusting in the heater’s tubes, the process gas enter the process gas heater (PG-601-B) through the convective zone and then it goes into the radiant cold zone and finally to the radiant hot zone the gas is collected at the heater transference line at a temperature between 910-930°C. At this point the process gas is prepared to be partially combusted with oxygen, to initiate another cycle of pre reforming, reforming and reduction.

## B. REDUCTION CIRCUIT EQUIPMENT FUNCTION

1.  Reactor (RE-621-D)

> The HYL reactor is a continuous heterogeneous gas–solid type. Its function is to maintain the solid and gas in temperature and pressure conditions so the reduction reactions and (in the particular case of the HYL 4M plant) the reforming and carburization reactions, take place.
>
> The reactor is made of carbon steel plate, the upper part is a semi spherical shaped followed by a cylindrical section, and they connect to another cylindrical section with larger diameter to end in a conical section. With the exception of the conical section, the inner side is covered with insulating material, refractory concrete and refractory brick.
>
> The HYL 4M is divided in two zones: reduction zone and lower zone. The reduction zone is composed by a cylindrical shaped part of 5.5 m inner diameter and 5.607 m of height, and a 4.556 m height conical section with an internal diameter that increase 2% in relation with the vertical up to the process gas inlet. Inside is covered with insulating concrete VSL-50 and refractory brick KX-99.
>
> The reducing gas inlet is located at the bottom part of the reduction zone, which is distributed by a ring made of refractory bricks KX-99 and it is called plenum, it has 124 rectangular exits symmetrically distributed at the plenum interior with and injection area of 1.69 m<sup>2</sup>.
>
> The bottom zone is the conical section located at the bottom part of the reactor, immediately below the reduction plenum. The inner surface of the cone is not thermally isolated but it has four jackets cooled by water externally to avoid hot spots. The discharge rotary valve is located at the end of this zone of the reactor; this valve controls the production rate. Also in this zone the reactor has two cluster breakers to promote the solid flow inside the reactor when problems may occur.
>
> The reactor of the HYL 4M plant is shown in the figure 3. In this figure the main zones and principal connections are identified, as well as the capacity of each zone. Module 4 shows more detail of the reactor.

2.  Heat recuperator (PG-614-C)

> The function of this equipment is to take advantage of the energy of the exhaust gas that leaves the reduction zone to pre heat the process gas from 70°C-80°C to 250ºC-260°C before this gas enters the heater and at the same time, to cool the exhausted gas (reduction outlet) from 360° to 270ºC.
>
> Heat recuperator PG-614-C is a shell-tubes type heat exchanger, the exhaust gas is feed through the 660 tubes of 51.25 mm D.I., while the process gas enters through the shell side with nine baffles to take the sensible heat of the exhaust gas, on the shell process gas side it has a pre heat section to avoid condensation at the exhaust gas outlet which would cause corrosion by the formation of carbonic gases.
>
> The heat recuperator is made of steel plate SA-312 Gr. T-22 with 2.25 % Cr and 1.0 % Mo. Internally is isolated with refractory concrete and refractory brick. Figure 4 represents this equipment.

3.  Quench orifice (PG-611-C)

> The exhaust gas after passing through the heat recuperator PG-614-C, enters the orifice cooler PG-611-C, where its temperature goes from 270 to 105ºC. Here the cooling takes place with a direct water injection to the gas stream.

The orifice cooler PG-611-C is an arrangement of two concentric tubes, the shell tubes is made of carbon steel, of 1067 mm external diameter and the tubes is made of stainless steel 304 and has 965 mm external diameter.

## C. REDUCTION GAS COMPRESSOR PG-671-J DESCRIPTION

The compressor PG-671-J is a two-impeller, helical-lobe, axial flow, rotary positive displacement dry type. The impeller profiles are mainly semi-circular with modifications to produce a pure rolling motion between adjacent surfaces. The power impeller (male) has four lobes; the secondary (female) impeller has six matching cavities. Outside diameters are identical but the rotative speed of the female impeller is two-thirds of the male.

Helical timing gears maintain a synchronized relationship between the lobes so that the impellers rotate without metal to metal contact at all times. Power is transmitted to the secondary impeller through the cushioning of the medium being compressed; the timing gears transmit only a negligible part of the total input horsepower. Positive positioning of the impellers is maintained by precision sleeve-type radial bearings and heavy duty thrust bearings.

Since there is no metal to metal contact within the compression chamber, no lubrication of the Impellers is needed.

The Impellers rotate in such a way that the Lobes and mating cavities part on the intake end and close on the discharge end. The parting of the lobes creates an intake suction which causes the interlobal space to be filled with gas. Further rotation isolates this space from the intake, and the intermeshing of the helical lobes reduces its volume gradually compressing the gas. As the leading edges of the lobes pass the stationary edges which form the discharge opening in the casing, the gas flows into the discharge line under the positive action of the intermeshing lobes. The closing action is completed and the interlobal space is reduced to zero before passing the discharge opening thereby avoiding trapped pockets. It also produces a practically continuous and even delivery of gas flow.

The construction of bearings and seals used in the compressor is such that any leakage of lubricant from the bearings or seals would normally be to the atmosphere and not into the compressor chamber.

The compressor housings are jacketed for water cooling. The rotors are cooled by oil flowing through the hollow rotor shafts.

## E. COMPRESSOR OPERATION PRINCIPLES PG-671-J

During the reduction compressor PG-671-J normal operation, one of the parameters that must be monitored, as much in field as in control room is the compressor discharge temperature, which is normally around 94ºC. If the temperature is above this value, is an indication that the molecular weight of the suction gas is increasing or the gas temperature is increasing at suction. If the temperature reaches 105ºC, the compressor will be automatically shutdown.

On the other side, high drop pressure at the filters is a parameter that should be supervised because it should not exceed 0.3 kg/cm<sup>2</sup>. Values higher than this are a clear indication that the operational filters are dirty and for this reason the compressor suction gas is decreasing.

Considering that the compressors are in constant movement is very important that the lubrication and cooling system are constantly checked along with the displacement of the vibrations.

The reduction compressor PG-671-J is rotatory, of helical lobes, of the positive displacement type. These compressors usually have a constant volume. The variable pressure machines can be found of flows from 20m<sup>3</sup>/min to 350m<sup>3</sup>/min with pressures up to 2 kg/cm<sup>2</sup> at one stage compressors and with higher pressures at two stages compressors.

The compression is carried out inside the machine as a result of the gas volume decrease, which is compressed between the rotors and the outlet plate.

This type of machines is equipped with antifriction radial type bearing as well as with thrust bearings for the axial positioning of the rotors. Lubrication is carried out by a pressurized oil system using a pump from its respective console.

1.  Control loop description

    1.  Heater process gas flow control

> The function of this control loop is to regulate the process gas flow going into the heater according to the production rate and the established conditions, as well as, to protect equipment such as the heater and compressor from damage due to flow variations.
>
> To regulate the process gas flow to the heater, the control valve PDV-67103 is used, this valve recycles the excess of gas to the compressor discharge to the process gas quench tower PG-631-E inlet and it does it under the following scheme:
>
> The operator adjusts the control loop with a certain flow by the process conditions feeding a set point at the FIC-67103. This controller compares the set point with the process variable (PV) coming from the FY-62212.
>
> The FY-62212 corresponds to the process gas calculation at the humidifier PG-622-E outlet, compensated by humidity, for this case the block is fed by the following points:

- FY-62213 Humidifier, inlet compensated process gas flow P1.

- TT-62210 Humidifier, temperature outlet P2.

- PI-62212 Humidifier process gas pressure outlet P3.

- FY-62201 Humidifier process gas humidity outlet P4.

> The FY-62212 receives the compensated process gas flow at the humidifier inlet (FY-62213 according to conditions TI-62213, GI-62213, PI-62213 y FI-62213), adapts the calculation to the quenching outlet conditions (TT-62210, PI-62212) as well as adding the volume of the water steam that is added to the stream FY-62201.
>
> The FIC-67103 receives the process variable (PV) and compares it with the set point which generates a direct signal that is sent to the FY-67103, which is the block that controls the compressor recirculation, its affected by an advance signal coming from the PY-62116 with the objective of protecting the heater for thermometry (START-TICS-601B and TIC-60116) and it also takes into account the reactor outlet pressure (PIC-62116).
>
> The FY-67103 is the block that controls the compressor recirculation, is a low value selector that chooses between:
>
> FIC-67103 Modified signal that represents the heater flow demand.
>
> TIC-62115 Protection signal to high temperature at reactor outlet.
>
> VIC-67103 Protection signal to the compressor and motor from high vibrations.
>
> PDIC Protection signal to the compressor from a high pressure and high suction differential.
>
> A signal is taken from the selector which commands directly to the valve, once all the variables have been recollected.
>
> A graphic representation of this control loop is show in figure 34.
>
> Examples:

- A 171,000 NCMH flow is being controlled at the FIC-67103 which operates in automatic mode, but according to the variation process the gas flow raises to 175,000 NCMH, this situation is shown in figure 35.

> One or more alterations are produced in the gas that leaves the CO<sub>2</sub> (VPSA) adsorption system; this is seen as compensated flow variation from the functional block FY-62213, which generates a higher signal output than before.
>
> Functional block FY-62212 receives a high signal, and supposing that the conditions of pressure (PI-62212) and temperature (TT-62210) at the humidifier outlet are maintained, this block also generates a high signal output, this signal is a process variable (PV) to controller FIC-67103.

<img src="images/image36.png" style="width:9.15486in;height:5.72431in" />

**FIGURE 34 PROCESS GAS FLOW CONTROL TOWARDS THE REACTOR**

> The FIC-67103 compares the received signal (PV) with the set point value (SP), as the deviation increases, the controller requires less, generating a low signal than before. This signal doesn’t have to be modified by the low selector block PY-62116 which protects the thermometry of the heater and the system pressure.
>
> The low signal arrives until the high selector block FY-67103, receives a lower value signal than the one coming from the FIC-67103, but even so, is higher than the one coming from the TSR, vibrations and compressor differential; because these ones just act at risk values.
>
> The high block selector FY-67103 will control the process gas flow sending a signal to the valve PDV-67103 to opens and so more process gas is re-circulated and therefore, process gas flow to the heater decreases.
>
> Figure 35 show this example.

- Process gas flow to the reactor is normal, but suddenly the reduction outlet temperature increases due to problems on the discharge system; it goes from 430ºC, which is the set point, to 480ºC, at this moment the control loop takes the next action: (See figure 36).

> The temperature increment is detected by the TE-62115 and the high signal is sent to the transmitter TT-62115 which acts through the controller TIC-62115. The TIC-62115 has direct control action; it will send a high signal to the high selector block FY-67103 and this one simultaneously to the signal inverter.

<img src="images/image37.png" style="width:8.95in;height:5.64653in" />

**FIGURE 35 PROCESS GAS FLOW CONTROL TOWARDS THE REACTOR EXAMPLE**

<img src="images/image38.png" style="width:9.27917in;height:5.82083in" />

**FIGURE 36 PROCESS GAS FLOW CONTROL TOWARDS THE REACTOR EXAMPLE**

> The control valve gets the low signal and opens; therefore the flow going to the reactor decreases, to prevent solid formation.

- The control operates normally but due to an excessive humidity in the process gas going to the compressor, the Dollinger filter gets saturated and the pressure differential between the compressor suction and discharge increases. Control takes the next action: (See figure 36).

> The increment is detected by the PDT-67103 which sends an increased signal to the controller PDIC-67103. The PDIC-67103 has a direct action control, reason why it will generate a high signal. The valve PDV-67103 increases its opening percentage when it receives these signals. When the valve opens, more flow is re-circulated from the discharge to the suction in order to adjust the pressure differential and avoid compressor damage. The last 2 examples are illustrated at figure 36.

2.  VPSA plant process gas pressure control.

> The function of this control loop is to keep a constant pressure of the exhaust gas at the inlet of the VPSA plant, by-passing the exhaust gas directly to the humidifier PG-622-E inlet, through control valve FV-62215. (See figure 37).

It’s considered that all the process gas is fed to the reactor and passes through both the VPSA plant and the absorption system; and under this condition the control valve PV-62215 is closed, but when pressure increases at the VPSA plant inlet it is an indication that the adsorption system capacity is being decreased and that at some point it will be out of service.

<img src="images/image39.png" style="width:6.10833in;height:5.99028in" />

**FIGURE 37 PROCESS GAS PRESSURE CONTROL TO VPSA PLANT**

> Under the concept that in some cases the plant can operate without adsorption system, when the pressure increases at the inlet line of the VPSA unit, part of this flow is divided, or in the full shut down case, according to the set point, the by-pass control valve PV-6225 opens a 100%. This flow is compensated with pressure and it is indicated at the FI-62215.
>
> The control loop has the next elements and the sequence is the following:
>
> At the K.O drum PG-622-F outlet, a pressure signal is taken and it’s sent to the pressure transmitter PT-62215, this transmitter sends a signal to the PI-62215 and from this the two signals leave: one to compensation flow pressure at FY-62215 and, to the pressure indicator controller PIC-62215.
>
> The PIC-62215 is a direct action controller and after comparing the process variable (PV) with the set point established, it sends a correction action to the control valve PV-62215.
>
> This control loop is represented in figure 37.

3.  Make up natural gas control to reduction circuit

> The function of this control loop is to maintain the methane % concentration in the process gas at the reactor outlet with the control valve FV-62214, which feeds a natural gas flow to the circuit, as make up for any loss, according to the following principle: (See figure 38).
>
> At the venturi scrubber PG-630-F outlet, a sample of the exhaust gas is taken by the methane analyzer AT-63000 which sends a signal to the methane indicator controller AIC-63000. The AIC-63000 is a reverse acting controller and according to set point fixed a signal outlet is sent to the FY-62214A.
>
> On the other hand, at the make up natural gas line the flow is measured with the flow-meter FE-62214 and the signal goes to the functional block FY-62214 which compensates the flow with pressure and temperature. The fixed flow signal is sent to the FIC-62214 as a process variable, and according to the set point value, modulates an outlet signal that goes to the FY-62214A. The FIC-62214 is an inverse action controller.
>
> Finally, the FY-62214A receives the pressure control signal PIC-62116A, because in case that the pressure drops in the reduction circuit the flow of natural gas helps to maintain the system pressure as long as the methane concentration doesn’t exceeds the values allowed at the AIC-63000.
>
> The FY-62214A it’s a low signal selector and will take the lower value from the controllers FIC-62214, AIC-63000 and PIC-62116A to send it to the control valve FV-62214. The valve FV-62214 works with air supply to open reason why when receiving a smaller signal the tendency of the valve will be to close, and vice versa, when detecting a greater pneumatic signal the valve opens.
>
> This control loop is given in figure 38.

4.  Nitrogen make up control to reduction circuit

> The function of this control loop is to maintain the methane concentration at the reactor process gas outlet, with a nitrogen injection (See figure 39).
>
> Methane % signal in the gas stream is received from the AT-63000 by the controller AIC-62298-A, which has a direct control action and according to the set point generates an outlet signal and sends it to the FY-62298-A.
>
> The same FY-62298-A receives another signal coming from PIC-62299-A this signal measures the reactor pressure. This controller has an inverse control action.

<img src="images/image40.png" style="width:6.03889in;height:5.67361in" />

>
> The FIC-62298 is a reverse acting controller; its operation mode is in cascade, even though it can operate automatically with a set point fixed by the operator.
>
> This controller receives a compensated flow signal by pressure as a process variable and its outlet is a corrective action at the control valve FV-62298.
>
> The valve FV-62298 has air supply to open reason why when receiving a diminished signal the tendency will be to close, and vice versa.
>
> Figure 39 show this control loop.

> The function of this control loop is to maintain the humidity value between 4.5 and 5.5% by adjusting the temperature and taking into account the gas pressure at the humidifier PG-622-E outlet. (See figure 40).
>
> For this purpose water is used with a temperature between 85-90°C which comes from the process gas quench tower PG-631-E. Water injection is regulated by control valveTV-62210 and it works as following:

7.  Temperature control at the quench orifice PG-611-C outlet

> The function of this control loop is to control the gas temperature at the quench orifice PG-611-C outlet with a water injection through the valve FV-61101. The way to operate is as follows: (See figure 42).
>
> At normal operation the temperature at the quench orifice outlet is detected; between the venturi PG-630-L and the venturi scrubber PG-630-F, by the thermo-well TE-61101, the signal is sent by the transmitter TT-61101 to the TIC-61101.

> The TIC-61101 is a controller with direct control action and it sends a corrective signal to the FY-61101. On other side the FY-61101 receives another signal from the TIC-61400 which measures the temperature of the reactor venting line, and this line leads the gas to the stack RE-614-F. The TIC-61400 is also a controller with direct control action and this one will take control when the reactor vent is being used.
>
> The FY-61101 is a high signal selector, when the reactor venting activates it takes the control signal from the TIC-61400 and when the venting reestablishes it takes the control of the TIC-61101. Usually the two controllers have the same value as a set point.

12. Reactor pressure control

> The function of this control loop is to maintain the reactor pressure stable by sending a flow of exhaust process gas to the tail gas header; to be used as fuel. This is done with control valves PV-62116A and B.

This valve is manipulated by the controller PIC-62116 in a shared rate of 20 and 80%, in such way that when 20% exists at the controller the valve will be a 100% open and from this moment, with the PIC-62116 outlet bigger than 20% the control will initiate by mean of the valve PV-62116B.
>
> The pressure signal is taken from the reactor and it is sent by the transmitter PT-62116 to the controller PIC-62116. The PIC-62116 generates an outset signal and sends it to the HIC-62116A and HIC-62116B; which according to the function principle will take the signal and will send it to the valves PV-62116 A y B.
>
> This control loop is show at figure 47.

# IV. REDUCTION CIRCUIT OPERATION

<a id="reduction-circuit-start-up"></a>
## A. REDUCTION CIRCUIT START UP

1.  Start up previous activities

    1.  Make sure there are in existence “F” keys to open and close valves.

    2.  Make sure that the level in the oil tank of the compressor lubricating unit and motor are in operative range.

    3.  Make sure that the stand by oil pump of the compressor and motor lubrication units are operable.

    4.  If some repairs have been carried out, check that the pipes, valves and instrumentation are all installed.

    5.  Put the principal lubricating oil pump in service, making sure that the flow is correct and the pressure is the required, and that the oil temperature is between 35ºC and 40ºC.

    6.  Verify that the alarm indications and oil lubricating system shut down are not present, if any of these are on; ask the instrumentation department to verify the problem.

    7.  Maintain the oil lubricating system pressure at 1.8 kg/cm<sup>2</sup> minimum.

    8.  Manually turn the compressor to assure that the rotors are not stuck.

    9.  Open the lateral valves of the gas compressor circulation valve PDV-67103, and open the valve a 100%. The compressor suction and discharge valves must be fully open.

    10. Open the inlet and outlet valves of the recycle gas filters PG-671-G1/2.

    11. Drain the K.O. drum PG-671-F, PG-622-F, as well as the gas filter PG-671-G1/2.

    12. Verify that there are no condensates inside the compressor, by opening the drain valves.

    13. Open the cooling water at the compressor jacket and the motor; verify the outlet flow on both.

    14. Put in service the level controllers for: Separator PG-630-F, process gas quench tower PG-631-E. Establish water seal in the humidifier PG-622-E and K.O. drum PG-650-F. Establish equipment cooling water through the after cooler PG-671-C.

In an initial start up, the VPSA adsorption system won’t be immediately at service, reason why in this case you must assure the availability of the by-pass system manually manipulating the valve PV-62215.

2.  Reduction circuit start up

> Before starting this stage, it is necessary that the points seen in the previous section have been covered.

1.  Process gas heater start up and reactor charge

> Initially start up the heater PG-601-B and at the same time starts the reactor charge, the initial temperature rate is at 70ºC/hr with radiant temperature reference and the reactor charging is at a rate of 150 ton/hr. See details at module 02 corresponding to the heater and 03 of the charge and discharge of reactor.

2.  Reduction compressor start up

> At the 6th hour of the circuit start up, the reduction compressor is put on service under the next procedure:

- Press the start up button and verify that the compressor gets to normal operation speed. In there’s any strange noise, shut down the compressor.

- Maintain the oil temperature between 40 and 45ºC opening or closing the water outlet valve of the heat exchanger.

- Verify that the lubrication oil pressure is being controlled above 1.8 kg/cm<sup>2</sup> by means of the principal lubrication pump.

- Make sure that the differential pressure between oil to seals and suction and discharge gas pressure is 1.0 psi (0.0703 kg/cm<sup>2</sup>).

- Verify that the pressure drop through the Dollinger filters be less than 0.3 kg/cm<sup>2</sup>.

- At the compressor start up establish a process cooling water flow through the quench orifice PG-650-C and put in operation the K.O. drum level controller PG-650-F. Because at the beginning the process gas will be by-passed by the dummy quench line. Keep the valve FV-65003 closed and the FV-65002 opened.

- Adjust the separator water level PG-630-F, to the venturi PG-631-L, to the process gas quench tower PG-631-E and put in service the humidifier PG-622-E.

> For this moment, the water valve to the quench orifice PG-611-C must be ready to start operation but must remain closed. Make sure that it is really closed.

- Stay 5 minutes at the compressor area after the compressor has been put in service, verifying that there are no alarms at the control panel and check that there are no strange noises. In case that the compressor shuts down, verify the cause and wait at least 10 minutes before re-starting it.

- Control the gas flow to the heater with the recirculation valve PDV-67103 according to the start up program.

> From the reduction compressor start up, the reference in temperature increment at the process gas heater will be at the riser with more temperature, it will be indicated at the TI-60115 at a rate of 70ºC/h until reaching a temperature of 650ºC. When reaching this temperature the increment rate will be at 100ºC/h.

1.  Start up of the natural gas injection to the reduction circuit

> The natural gas injection to the circuit must be carried out when the outlet temperature of the process gas heater is above 700ºC and the flow will be according to the start up program.
>
> To open the natural gas injection valves it is necessary not to have activated anyone of the mentioned conditions at the protection section for the valves XSV-62214 and FV-62214.
>
> When the natural gas injection to the reduction circuit starts up all the nitrogen in the circuit must be purged, reason why at this moment the control valve of reactor pressure must be put in control PV-62116A/B.

2.  Oxygen injection at the transference line

> When the natural gas injection starts up, the specific gravity of the gas decreasing because the natural gas has minor specific gravity than the nitrogen, this data must be monitored.
>
> When having 0.85 at the gas gravity the oxygen injection can start up, assuring that the ignition was carried out.
>
> At this moment the pre-reforming reactions begin, and the temperature after the oxygen injection, as well as the gas composition, are parameters to keep under observation.
>
> The oxygen injection will be made according to the procedure established in the module 15, referring to partial combustion and the flow according to the established program.

3.  H<sub>2</sub>S injection to the reduction circuit

> The H<sub>2</sub>S injection to the circuit must be done once the natural gas injection has begun, and the concentration must be controlled at 20-25 ppm Vol. at the process gas heater inlet.
>
> The start up of the H<sub>2</sub>S injection system to the process gas heater is detailed at module 02 corresponding to the heater PG-601-B.

4.  Process gas through the reactor and discharge initiation.

> When reaching 910ºC at the TER the flow through the reactor begins.
>
> During this activity is important to take care of the water balance to the quench orifice PG-650-C and PG-611-C, in such way that at the end of this movement the control must be considered at PG-611-C and the water valve to the PG-650-C must be completely closed but available to be put in service at any required time. Verify that there are no leaks at the water line to the PG-650-C.
>
> Feeding the gas through the reactor must be done slowly to avoid considerable pressure and temperature changes at the circuit equipment. Take care of the gas balance with FI-65002 and FIC-67103 closing the valve FV-65002 and opening at the same time the FV-65003.
>
> When the gas goes through the reactor the ore reduction begins and the reduction outlet temperature (TSR) starts to increase. At first, the discharge will be turning the rotary valve at minimum speed (1 turn every 15 minutes). Next the control is carried out according to the TSR indication adjusting a temperature from 430 to 470ºC. This temperature is indicated at the TIC-62115.
>
> When all the conditions have been reached and the gas composition is stable, the expected TSR range will be between 430 and 470ºC.
>
> When all the reduction circuit conditions have been reached, the reactor purge gas can be used as fuel at the process gas heater.

5.  Star-up of the flow of process gas through the VPSA adsorption plant

> After the oxygen injection at the transference line, due to partial combustion and gas going through the reactor, the generation of H<sub>2</sub> y CO begins, but at the same time CO<sub>2</sub> is created and water steam as result of the combustion and reduction.
>
> In order to favor the concentration of reducing agents the CO<sub>2</sub> concentration must be reduced, so when reaching a 12 % process gas concentration, the gas must go through the VPSA plant controlling the concentration at 3.0 %.
>
> The procedure for the VPSA adsorption system start-up is described at module 07.
>
> NOTE: this procedure might have some variations such as reactor filling time, compressor start up hour, gas going through the reactor, among others. Nevertheless any variation of this type must be considered at the start up program.

During reduction circuit normal operation of the 4M plant, there are some operation conditions that must be controlled in order to maintain the stability of the process, this conditions are presented next:

> TER 1060-1085ºC
>
> FER 1665 NCM/ton FeT
>
> TSC 910-930°C
>
> IGNR 160 NCM/ton FeT
>
> O<sub>2</sub> 61.5 NCM/ton FeT

Under these circumstances the operator must set the control points at the corresponding controllers to obtain the following flows and temperatures:

> TIC-60150 1060°C-1085ºC.
>
> FIC-67103 160,000-165,000 NCMH.
>
> TIC-60116 910°C-930ºC.
>

>

<a id="reduction-circuit-normal-shut-down"></a>
## C. REDUCTION CIRCUIT NORMAL SHUT DOWN

A normal shut down of the reduction circuit is considered a scheduled shut down, for this shut down a procedure opposite to the start up is followed and the steps are these:

1.  Beginning of the decrease of flow and temperature

> At the first hour of the shut down the flow and temperature of the reactor begin to decrease; this will be done according to schedule.
>
> The temperature decrease at the heater will have a rate of 100ºC/h taking the TIC-60116 as reference which corresponds as the heater outlet temperature.
>
> When initiating this activity the TSR will be taken as base of the control for the discharge because the TER also decreases.

2.  Stop the oxygen injection at the transference line

> When reaching 900ºC in the reactor inlet temperature (TER) the oxygen injection at the transference line must stop.
>
> Changes in the gas composition will occur when oxygen injection stops due to this changes other activities must be done at the same time. These activities are the following:

3.  By-pass the process gas through the dummy quench line.

> Once the oxygen injection at the transference line has stopped a by-pass of gas through the dummy quench line must be done, at the same time, a water balance at the quench orifice PG-650-C and PG-611-C must be done, in such way that at the end of this activity the temperature control will be at PG-650-C and the water feed line to PG-611-C must be completely closed.
>
> The gas balance must be considered too, in this case with the flow indicators FI-65002 and FIC-67103. This change is made with the valves FV-65002 and FV-65003, at the end the FV-65003 is closed and the FV-65002 open.
>
> At the same time the reactor charge system is stopped, the ducts must be empty and the injection of cold gas must be available if it is required.

4.  VPSA adsorption plant stop

> When the gas process stops passing through of the reactor and there is no oxygen injection at the transference line, no more CO<sub>2</sub> is created, which is why at this moment the VPSA can be stopped.
>
> In this case the by-pass is made with the valve PV-62215. See details of this activity at module 07.

5.  Stop the natural gas injection to the circuit

> Once the oxygen injection is suspended and the process gas flow is by passed via dummy quench, the injection of natural gas can be stopped and the make up to maintain reduction circuit pressure, can be done with Nitrogen.
>
> This activity is known as “to bottle the circuit” and the valves that remain closed are the natural gas valve FV-62214 and the reactor pressure PV-62116 A/B, and the nitrogen make up is done with the FV-62298.
>
> Before bottling the circuit it must be stopped the use of tail gas as fuel gas at the process gas heater and make the necessary adjustments with natural gas.
>
> At this time the H<sub>2</sub>S injection to the circuit is suspended, because the reduction circuit is almost 100% nitrogen.

6.  Reduction compressor shut down

> When reaching 650ºC at the heater outlet (TSC) the decrease will continue with a rate of 70ºC/h with reference at the TI-60115.
>
> When reaching 370°C in the heater exit (TSC), the compressor will stop and the temperature decrements in the heater will be now with reference to the radiant temperature and it’s indicated at TI-60113 and TI-60114.
>
> When the reduction compressor stops, the water flow to the quench orifice PG-650-C and the venturi PG-631-L should be completely closed.

7.  Reactor discharge system

> Once bottling the circuit the reactor discharge should be at the highest rate possible and the quality of the DRI should be monitored to separate the one with good characteristics (metallization and carbon).
>
> The discharge rate in this case is directly related to the pneumatic transport availability and the external cooling system.

8.  Depressurize the reduction circuit

> When the reactor is empty the reduction circuit can be depressurized, before that the gas separator PG-630-F, the packed bed cooler PG-631-L and the humidifier PG-622-E must be out of service and with a water seal.

9.  Reduction circuit safety purge

> Before opening any equipment man hole; it must be sure that there are no fuel gases at the reduction circuit, which is why, is recommended that after depressurizing, purge with nitrogen. After every purge, check with a digital gas analyzer at different points.
>
> Once no explosive mixture is detected, the blind flanges are placed to isolate the equipments that will take part during the shut down.
>
> NOTE: at the shut down as well as the start up, the sequence of some activities may differ, and they should be considered at the shut down schedule.
